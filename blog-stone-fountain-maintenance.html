Stone fountain maintenance.

A little attention, done regularly, keeps a stone fountain looking beautiful and running quietly for years. Here is a routine that works in Indian conditions.

Care GuideBy Chambal Fountain · KotaUpdated 21 November 20267 min read

Most fountain problems are small problems that were left alone — a low water level, a blocked intake, a layer of grit in the basin. A simple routine prevents nearly all of them.

The weekly check (two minutes)

Once a week, look at the fountain and check three things: the water level, the flow and the general cleanliness of the basin. Recirculating fountains lose water to evaporation and splash, so topping up is normal. If the flow has weakened, check the water level before anything else — a pump running close to empty is the most common cause.

  • Top up with clean water so the pump stays fully submerged.
  • Remove leaves, insects and debris from the basin.
  • Check that the stream falls where it should and nothing is blocked.

Cleaning the basin and pump

Every four to eight weeks, give the fountain a more thorough clean. Switch off and unplug the pump before touching anything. Lift out the pump, rinse the intake and impeller housing under clean water, and clear any grit or hair-like debris. Wipe the basin with a soft brush or cloth.

For the stone itself, plain water and a soft brush are usually enough. Avoid harsh acids, strong bleach and high-pressure jets — these can mark or erode a stone surface and disturb the finish. If your fountain has a particular finish, follow the care advice given for it.

ImportantAlways disconnect power before cleaning or handling the pump. Never run a dry pump — it can be damaged quickly.

Water quality: the quiet culprit

Clean water is easier on the pump and kinder to the stone. In dusty conditions or under trees, debris builds up faster. Refilling with fresh water periodically helps, and any water treatment should be checked against your pump and finish before use. Splashes that dry on the stone can leave mineral marks, so wiping them while the fountain is running is worth the effort.

Season-by-season routine

Summer

Evaporation is highest, so check the water level more often. Warm water and sunlight can encourage algae; keep the water moving and the basin clear. A little shade helps if the fountain is exposed all day.

Monsoon

Rain can overflow a basin and bring in mud and leaves. Clear debris after heavy rain and check that the pump intake is not blocked. Outdoor electrical connections should be protected and inspected.

Winter

In most of India, fountains can run through winter with normal care. In places where temperatures fall below freezing, standing water and pumps need protection — drain and store the pump as advised for your climate, and do not let ice form in the basin.

Start of a new season

Give the whole fountain a full clean before regular use resumes: basin, pump, tubing and stone. It is the best time to spot small wear before it becomes a repair.

When to call for help

If the pump is clean and submerged but still has no flow, if there is a leak, or if a part needs replacement, it is better to ask than to force it. Send a short video of the problem to the team and they can suggest the next step.

Choosing a fountain that is easier to care for

Maintenance starts at the design stage. A basin that is easy to reach, a simple flow path and a finish that suits your environment all make daily care simpler. If you are still choosing a fountain, these points are worth discussing before the design is fixed.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I clean my stone fountain?

Check weekly and top up as needed. Clean the basin and pump every four to eight weeks, more often under trees or in dusty areas. A full seasonal clean is a good habit.

What is the safest way to clean a stone fountain?

Switch off and unplug the pump. Remove loose debris, then wipe the stone with a soft brush or cloth and plain water. Avoid harsh acids, strong bleach and high-pressure jets.

Why is my fountain pump losing flow?

Usually a low water level, blocked intake, clogged filter or debris on the impeller. Check these in order before assuming the pump has failed — a simple clean often fixes it.

Can stone fountains stay outside in monsoon or winter?

Yes, with extra attention. Clear debris after heavy rain, protect electrical connections, and in freezing climates drain standing water and protect or store the pump.

How do I stop algae and mineral marks?

Keep water moving, reduce harsh direct sun where possible, clean regularly, and use treatments only if suitable for your pump and finish. Wipe splashes before they dry.

Care at a glance
  • Weekly: level, flow, debris
  • Every 4–8 weeks: pump + basin
  • Monsoon: clear after heavy rain
  • Winter: protect from freezing
  • Always: power off before cleaning
